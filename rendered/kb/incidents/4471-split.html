How to tell them apart

Read the date in the report, then look up the tenant and how the report came in.

  • Day and month swapped (7 April shows as 4 July). Came in by app, email or the booking-v2 form: the parser problem. The tenant writes the day first and the shared parser reads month first.
  • Day and month swapped, but it came in through the booking-v3 form: the hint problem. The form prints MM/DD/YYYY, people type what it says, and the form reads it day first. It looks exactly like the parser problem, so only the channel tells them apart.
  • One day off (Tuesday shows as Monday, or two people see different days): the shift problem. Swapping day and month can’t produce this. It points to time zones, which is an inference until someone reproduces it.

Why one ticket hid three

  • The subject doesn’t separate them. #4471 took in “wrong day” reports under many different subjects, and the same subject turns up in all three problems. Matching on subject (decision 0001) and “when unsure, merge” (triage) both pull them together.
  • Intake tags each report from the reporter’s browser, not the tenant. On #4471 the tags disagree with the cause both ways, so neither the tag nor the subject can split the ticket. The tenant directory and the channel can.
  • The export repeats reports where its pages overlap. Count by id: the table above does.

What to do

  • Keep the queued parser fix on #1.
  • Fix the booking-v3 date hint: #2.
  • Reproduce the one-day shift across time zones: #3. One reporter says they stopped reporting it, so its count is a floor.
  • When a big ticket’s table shows more than one cause, split the ticket before marking it known.
